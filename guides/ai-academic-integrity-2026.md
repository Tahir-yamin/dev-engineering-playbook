# AI Academic Integrity & Detection Safety Guide (2026)

## Overview
As of 2026, Q1 journals and major publishers (Elsevier, Springer, Nature, IEEE) have transitioned from "prohibition" to "transparency." While AI detectors (Turnitin, GPTZero) have become more accurate, they are now used as **probabilistic flags** rather than definitive evidence.

## How to Write Safely for Q1 Journals (General)

### 1. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Core
Never submit raw AI output. Use AI for:
- Literature mapping (SciSpace).
- Grammar polishing (Paperpal).
- Structural outlines.

**Do NOT use AI for:**
- Original data interpretation.
- Novel hypothesis generation (without significant human verification).
- Fabricating citations (always use Scite.ai to verify).

### 2. Passing Turnitin AI Detection (2025-2026 Trends)
Turnitin's 2026 model looks for "Perplexity" (predictability) and "Burstiness" (variation).

- **Increase Perplexity**: Avoid generic transitions like "Moreover," "Furthermore," or "In conclusion." Use domain-specific connectors that reflect a researcher's thought process (e.g., "This specific anomaly in the spectrometer data suggests...").
- **Increase Burstiness**: Manually mix sentence structures. AI tends to produce sentences of similar length. Introduce short, declarative "insight" sentences followed by complex "explanation" sentences.
- **Intellectual Hesitation**: AI often speaks in certainties. Real scholars use "hedging" (e.g., *may, possibly, implies, appears to be*). Adding this makes the text significantly more "human" to a detector.

### 3. Handling Plagiarism Reports
- **Text Matching**: Use AI-assisted paraphrasing (Quillbot/Claude) to rewrite common phrases, but ensure the **original meaning** is meticulously preserved.
- **Proper Attribution**: Even if AI helped you rewrite a section, the original idea must still be cited. Turnitin 2026 is excellent at identifying "patchwork" plagiarism where ideas from different sources are stitched together.

## Sector-Specific Policies: Engineering & Energy (IEEE/Elsevier)

### IEEE Guidelines (2026)
- **Author Prohibition**: AI tools *cannot* be listed as an author.
- **Experimental Logic**: You **cannot** use LLMs to generate the core experimental design or the primary code logic of your contribution.
- **LLM Text**: Several IEEE societies prohibit the use of LLM-generated text *unless* the study itself is about LLMs. Even "polishing" should be disclosed.
- **Disclosure**: Mandatory in the Acknowledgments section.

### Elsevier Applied Energy (2026)
- **Permitted**: Language enhancement, readability improvement, code debugging.
- **Prohibited**: Scientific interpretation of results, generating raw data, or creating conclusions.
- **Images**: Generative AI cannot be used to create figures or graphical abstracts unless explicitly part of the research method.

## AI Disclosure Statement Templates

### General (Nature/Springer)
> "The authors acknowledge the use of [Tool Name] for [Task, e.g., language refinement and citation verification]. The conceptual framework and data analysis were conducted entirely by the authors. All generated suggestions were reviewed and validated by the primary author."

### Engineering/Technical (IEEE)
> "The authors used [Tool, e.g., Grammarly/Overleaf AI] exclusively for grammatical refinement and LaTeX error correction. No AI tools were used to generate experimental results, code logic, or scientific conclusions."
